# The Thesis

Tokenomics are announced after funds are already raised. Vesting schedules declared one day, amended the next. Token liquidity pulled and dumped days after launch. Supply expanded after contributors committed capital. Many projects declare their token economy after the IDO — and even those that declare it before remain subject to change. The same party that controls the treasury executes distributions — unilaterally, without audit, without consequence. Nothing is fixed. Nothing is binding. And being in control is not the problem — the problem is that nothing is verifiable, nothing is immutable, and extracting on-chain data to confirm what was promised takes a level of expertise most participants do not have. The rules of the game are written by whoever holds the keys, and they are written whenever it is most convenient for them.

This asymmetry did not happen by accident. It was structurally permitted. And it forced every participant — retail, institutional, foundation, developer — into the same position: speculating on whether the rules would be honored, rather than making decisions based on what the rules actually are.

The result is a primary market where winning is a byproduct of secret advantages, not strategy. Where the informed few extract value from the uninformed many. Where transparency is a marketing claim, not a technical guarantee.

**Perfect Information Labs is built on a different premise.**

In game theory, a game of perfect information is one where every participant sees the complete state of the game before making a move. No hidden variables. No private information. No asymmetric advantage. Every decision made on the same foundation of complete, verifiable fact.

That is what Web3 was supposed to be. The blockchain is a public ledger. Every transaction is visible. Every contract is auditable. The infrastructure for perfect information already exists — it just was never enforced at the protocol level where it matters most.

Exhibition and Onchain Tokenomist enforce it.

**Exhibition** ensures that the state you see at the beginning of a launch is mathematically guaranteed at the end. Every parameter — funding target, token price, vesting schedule, liquidity allocation — declared on-chain before capital enters and immutable from that point forward. No discretionary execution. No post-launch amendments. Public contributors become the initial liquidity — automatically and atomically at launch completion. The hunt for initial liquidity is over before it begins. No broken agreements. No dependency on external market makers. And because that foundation is already live and verifiable on-chain, every subsequent listing — on a CEX, an external DEX, additional pairs, or deeper pools on Exhibition itself — is pursued from a position of strength, not desperation. The contract is the guarantee.

**Onchain Tokenomist** ensures that after the launch, the token economy is governed by the same standard. Every allocation, every vesting schedule, every distribution rule composed into a single on-chain vault — exactly as it lives in your documentation, exactly as your project intended. No spreadsheets. No operator trust. No off-chain source of truth. The vault is the source of truth.

Both protocols are pure tools. Exhibition imposes no mandatory liquidity percentage, no forced lock duration, no template. Onchain Tokenomist imposes no template either — you compose your entire token economy from scratch, in whatever structure your project demands. The only mandated rules are the launch duration and the liquidity finalization deadline — the minimum required to protect participants without restricting creators. Before creation, you are free to build anything. After creation, what you built is permanent. The protocol does not dictate how you build. It only guarantees that what you build cannot be silently changed afterward.

Together they close the full loop. There is no longer a moment in the lifecycle of a token where a participant must trust rather than verify.

**Every win becomes a function of strategy. Every loss becomes a function of strategy. The game becomes fair — not by promise, but by protocol.**
