# The Thesis

Tokenomics are announced after funds are already raised. Vesting schedules declared one day, amended the next. Token liquidity pulled and dumped days after launch. Supply expanded after contributors committed capital. Many projects declare their token economy after the IDO — and even those that declare it before remain subject to change. The same party that controls the treasury executes distributions — unilaterally, without audit, without consequence. Nothing is fixed. Nothing is binding. And being in control is not the problem — the problem is that nothing is verifiable, nothing is immutable, and extracting on-chain data to confirm what was promised takes a level of expertise most participants do not have. The rules of the game are written by whoever holds the keys, and they are written whenever it is most convenient for them.

This asymmetry did not happen by accident. It was structurally permitted. And it forced every participant — retail, institutional, foundation, developer — into the same position: speculating on whether the rules would be honored, rather than making decisions based on what the rules actually are.

The result is a primary market where winning is a byproduct of secret advantages, not strategy. Where the informed few extract value from the uninformed many. Where transparency is a marketing claim, not a technical guarantee.

This asymmetry is not just unfair — it is the single biggest barrier to mass adoption. When participants cannot trust that the rules are fixed, when insiders hold information the public does not, when transparency is a promise rather than a protocol-level enforcement — the rational response is to stay out. And most people do. Web3 promised a system where the ledger is public, the rules are visible, and no participant has a hidden advantage. That promise was never enforced where it matters most — at the point where capital meets a new token. The team holds cards the contributor never sees. The information asymmetry is not accidental — it is structural, and it has been the primary reason the people Web3 was supposed to serve the most have trusted it the least.

**Perfect Information Labs is built on a different premise.**

In game theory, a game of perfect information is one where every participant sees the complete state of the game before making a move. No hidden variables. No private information. No asymmetric advantage. Every decision made on the same foundation of complete, verifiable fact.

That is what Web3 was supposed to be. The blockchain is a public ledger. Every transaction is visible. Every contract is auditable. The infrastructure for perfect information already exists — it just was never enforced at the protocol level where it matters most.

Exhibition and Onchain Tokenomist enforce it.

**Exhibition** ensures that the state you see at the beginning of a launch is mathematically guaranteed at the end. Every parameter — funding target, token price, vesting schedule, liquidity allocation — declared on-chain before capital enters and immutable from that point forward. No discretionary execution. No post-launch amendments. Public contributors become the initial liquidity — automatically and atomically at launch completion. The hunt for initial liquidity is over before it begins. No broken agreements. No dependency on external arrangements before a token can trade. And because that foundation is already live and verifiable on-chain, every subsequent listing — on a CEX, an external DEX, additional pairs, or deeper pools on Exhibition itself — is pursued from a position of strength, not desperation. The contract is the guarantee.

**Onchain Tokenomist** ensures the token economy can meet the same standard. Every allocation, every vesting schedule, every distribution rule composable into a single on-chain vault — exactly as it lives in your documentation, exactly as your project intended. No spreadsheets. No operator trust. No off-chain source of truth. The vault is the source of truth. A project that commits its token economy on-chain before accepting public capital is a project that has nothing to hide.

Both protocols are pure tools. Exhibition imposes no mandatory minimum liquidity percentage, no min-max lock duration. Onchain Tokenomist imposes no template — you compose your exact token economy from scratch, in whatever structure your project demands. The only protocol-enforced boundaries are the soft cap minimum, the maximum launch duration, and the liquidity finalization deadline — the minimum required to protect participants without restricting creators.

Before creation, you are free to build anything. After creation, what you built is permanent. The protocols do not dictate how you build. They only guarantee that what you build cannot be silently changed afterward.

Together they close the full loop. There is no longer a moment in the lifecycle of a token where a participant must trust rather than verify.
